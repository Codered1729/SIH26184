## 1. Statutory Frameworks & Legal Foundations

SENTINEL is built from the ground up to comply strictly with India's criminal justice legislative framework enacted under the 2023 procedural codes, ensuring that all digital intelligence, preservation orders, and audit logs are admissible in judicial proceedings.

### 1.1 Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023

#### Section 105: Summons / Order for Production of Documents or Things
- **Statutory Mandate:** Empowers the officer in charge of a police station or an authorized cyber cell investigator to issue an order requiring the production of any document, digital record, or transactional artifact necessary for the investigation.
- **SENTINEL Integration:** Used at the ingestion stage to ingest attested bank ledger extracts and telecommunication CDR/IMEI logs.

#### Section 106: Attachment, Forfeiture, or Preservation of Property Derived from Criminal Activities
- **Statutory Mandate:** Provides explicit statutory authorization for the preservation, seizure, and attachment of property (including digital currency and bank balances) identified as proceeds of crime.
- **SENTINEL Integration:** Forms the legal backbone of SENTINEL's rapid-dispatch preservation orders to bank nodal officers.

#### Section 107(5): Proportionate Disputed-Amount Preservation Lien
- **Statutory Mandate:** Mandates that where an account holds funds in excess of the illicit proceeds, the attachment or freeze order shall be **strictly restricted to the disputed quantum** derived from the criminal activity, prohibiting blanket freezes of entire operational accounts.
- **SENTINEL Integration:**
  - Traditional Section 91 CrPC notices triggered unconstitutional blanket account freezes, blocking ₹5,00,000 for a ₹20,000 dispute.
  - SENTINEL programmatically calculates the exact disputed rupee quantum ($₹\text{Disputed}$), formatting the notice to instruct the bank nodal officer to place a **debit freeze strictly on the disputed amount**, leaving operational and merchant balances active.

### 1.2 Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023

#### Section 63(4): Admissibility of Electronic Records & Cryptographic Attestation
- **Statutory Mandate:** Replaces the former Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872. Mandates that any electronic record produced as evidence in court must be accompanied by a certificate signed by the person in lawful control of the computing device, verifying the cryptographic integrity and uncorrupted transmission of the electronic record.
- **SENTINEL Integration:**
  - Every preservation notice generated by SENTINEL automatically produces a **Section 63(4) BSA Hash Certificate**.
  - The certificate computes a deterministic SHA-256 digest over the complaint parameters, victim account, beneficiary account, UTR, timestamp, and nodal officer dispatch metadata:
    $$\text{BSA\_Hash} = \text{SHA256}(\text{UTR} \parallel \text{Timestamp} \parallel \text{Amount} \parallel \text{Account} \parallel \text{Officer\_ID})$$
  - The resulting cryptographic seal is permanently anchored into the append-only SQLite WAL ledger.

### 1.3 Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023

#### Principle of Data Minimization & Zero Raw PII Storage
- **Statutory Mandate:** Entities processing personal digital data must limit collection to the minimum required for the stated lawful purpose and prevent unauthorized disclosure.
- **SENTINEL Integration:**
  - Citizen Aadhaar numbers, phone numbers, and full bank account numbers are never stored in raw plaintext.
  - Accounts are tokenized using deterministic HMAC-SHA256 (`SBIN0001029:****3819`), preserving analytical relationship graphs while eliminating data breach exposure.
  - System complies with LEA statutory exemptions under Section 17(1)(c) of the DPDP Act for prevention, detection, and investigation of offenses.

### 1.4 Information Technology Act, 2000 (Amended 2008)
- **Section 43A:** Reasonable security practices and procedures for protecting sensitive personal data.
- **Section 66D:** Punishment for cheating by personation using computer resources (core charge under which cyber fraud complaints are registered).
- **Section 79:** Safe harbor and intermediary guidelines governing nodal bank cooperation.

## 2. Regulatory Directives & Institutional Sources

### 2.1 Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Directives & Circulars
1. **Master Direction – Frauds: Classification and Reporting by commercial banks and select FIs**  
   *Reference:* RBI/DBS.CO/FRAUD.BC.1/23.04.001/2021-22 (Updated 2023)  
   *Application:* Establishes mandatory 24-hour reporting timelines, Flash Reports to Central Fraud Registry (CFR), and nodal banking officer escalation trees.
2. **Framework for Limiting Customer Liability in Unauthorized Electronic Banking Transactions**  
   *Reference:* RBI/2017-18/15 (DBR.No.Leg.BC.78/09.07.005/2017-18)  
   *Application:* Defines the "zero liability" criteria when fraud is reported within 3 days, establishing the policy justification for automated police-bank interdiction.
3. **ATM Deployment & Cash Management Guidelines**  
   *Reference:* RBI Circular on ATM Cash Replenishment Norms (2020) and Security Measures (2022)  
   *Application:* Calibrates ATM cash dispensing velocities, cassette replenishment cadences, and maximum per-transaction withdrawal limits ($₹10,000$ to $₹40,000$).

## 4. Synthetic Data Generation Methodology & Statistical Calibration

Because live cybercrime complaint databases (CFCFRMS / NCRP) contain sensitive personal identifiable information (PII) restricted under the DPDP Act and Official Secrets Act, SENTINEL generates and trains on a **mathematically and stochastically calibrated 18,000-complaint synthetic corpus**:

1. **Geographic Grounding:**
   - 25 calibrated ATM clusters mapped to real geolocations across Maharashtra (Mumbai BKC, Andheri, Pune Hinjawadi, Shivajinagar, Nagpur Sitabuldi, Nashik CBS) and Gujarat (Surat Ring Road, Ahmedabad SG Highway, Vadodara Alkapuri).
   - District ATM densities calibrated against official RBI bank branch statistics.

2. **Temporal Smurfing Dynamics:**
   - Transaction timestamps sampled from non-homogeneous Poisson processes reflecting real diurnal cybercrime filing curves (peaks at 11:00–14:00 and 19:00–22:00 IST).
   - Golden window velocities calibrated against NPCI IMPS/UPI switch clearing times.

3. **Multi-Hop Graph Topology:**
   - Single-hop transfers: 41.8% of corpus (5,400 complaints with zero instant cashout).
   - 2-hop structuring: 24.5% of corpus.
   - 3-hop structuring: 14.3% of corpus.
   - 4+ hop structuring: 19.4% of corpus.
   - Cashout siphons stochastically bounded to realistic ATM per-card limits ($₹10,000$ to $₹40,000$).
